## What It Means

The **loop transfer function** is the open-loop product seen around a broken
feedback loop. In a unity-feedback SISO loop it is usually written as $L(s)=P(s)C(s)$.
The loop transfer $L$ is the central object for frequency-domain design because it
connects stability, margins, sensitivity, noise amplification, and bandwidth.

## Mathematical Form

For unity negative feedback,

$$
L(s)=P(s)C(s),\qquad
S(s)=\frac{1}{1+L(s)},\qquad
T(s)=\frac{L(s)}{1+L(s)}.
$$

Low-frequency high gain usually improves tracking and disturbance rejection. High
frequency gain usually amplifies sensor noise and unmodeled dynamics.

## Intuition

Loop design is a negotiation over frequency. You want enough gain where commands
and disturbances matter, enough phase margin near crossover, and enough roll-off
where the model and sensors become unreliable.

## How It Is Used

1. Form $L=PC$ from the plant and candidate controller.
2. Inspect crossover frequency, phase margin, gain margin, and resonance peaks.
3. Shape $L$ with lead, lag, PID, filters, or robust-control weights.
4. Recompute $S$, $T$, actuator effort, and noise paths.

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating high bandwidth as always better.
- Ignoring model uncertainty near and above crossover.
- Reading margins from $L$ without checking saturation and implementation delay.
