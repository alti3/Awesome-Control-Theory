## What It Means

**Biological models** describe feedback in living systems: homeostasis, gene
regulation, neural circuits, hormone regulation, immune response, and synthetic
biological networks. Biological examples show that feedback is not only an
engineered mechanism; it is also a recurring organizing principle in nature.

## Mathematical Form

Many biological feedback models use nonlinear state equations:

$$
\dot x=f(x,u,p),\qquad y=h(x),
$$

where $x$ may be concentration, population, membrane potential, or a physiological
state, and $p$ collects uncertain kinetic parameters.

## Intuition

Biological loops often combine feedback, saturation, delay, stochasticity, and
switching. A small-signal model can explain local regulation, while nonlinear
analysis is needed for oscillations, bistability, and threshold effects.

## How It Is Used

1. Identify states and conserved quantities from biology or chemistry.
2. Decide whether concentrations, populations, or probabilities are the right
   state variables.
3. Fit uncertain rates from data when first-principles parameters are unavailable.
4. Analyze equilibria, oscillations, robustness, and sensitivity.

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating parameters as precisely known when they are fitted indirectly.
- Applying linear conclusions across large concentration or population changes.
- Ignoring stochastic effects in small populations.
