## What It Means

**Computing and network models** apply feedback ideas to digital services and
communication systems. Examples include web-server resource management, admission
control, router protocols, and congestion control, where the controlled variables
may be queue length, throughput, latency, packet loss, or CPU allocation.

## Mathematical Form

A queue-like fluid approximation often has the form

$$
\dot q = a(t)-s(t,u),
$$

where $q$ is backlog, $a$ is arrival rate, and $s$ is service rate controlled by
resource allocation or admission decisions.

## Intuition

The same feedback problem appears with different units: measure congestion or
latency, decide whether to admit work or allocate resources, and act before queues
grow into outages. Delay and stochastic arrivals make these loops harder than their
simple models suggest.

## How It Is Used

1. Choose controlled variables such as latency, utilization, loss, or queue length.
2. Build a flow, queueing, or empirical model around the operating region.
3. Include measurement and actuation delays.
4. Combine feedback with logic for admission, overload protection, and scaling.

## Common Pitfalls

- Ignoring transport and measurement delay in congestion loops.
- Using average models when burstiness dominates tail latency.
- Optimizing utilization without a stability margin for queues.
