## What It Means

**Operational-amplifier models** describe high-gain electronic amplifiers used with
feedback to create precise gains, filters, integrators, and analog controller
elements. Op amps connect Black's feedback amplifier idea to modern control block
diagrams and implementation.

## Mathematical Form

An idealized inverting amplifier has a closed-loop gain set mostly by passive
components:

$$
\frac{V_o}{V_i}\approx -\frac{Z_2}{Z_1},
$$

when the open-loop gain is very large and the amplifier is not saturated.

## Intuition

High open-loop gain is made useful by feedback. The closed-loop behavior becomes
less sensitive to amplifier details and more determined by the feedback network,
until bandwidth, saturation, and slew-rate limits matter.

## How It Is Used

1. Model the amplifier open-loop gain and dominant bandwidth limits.
2. Choose feedback impedances for gain, integration, or filtering.
3. Check saturation, slew rate, and stability margins.
4. Translate analog realizations into equivalent block diagrams.

## Common Pitfalls

- Assuming infinite gain and bandwidth.
- Ignoring saturation and slew-rate limits.
- Forgetting that op-amp circuits can oscillate if feedback phase is wrong.
