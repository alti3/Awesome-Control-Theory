## What It Means

**Bode's relations** connect magnitude and phase for stable minimum-phase transfer
functions. In feedback design they explain why loop-shaping is constrained: you
cannot arbitrarily choose gain and phase independently, and reducing sensitivity in
one band tends to push sensitivity elsewhere.

## Mathematical Form

For many stable loops, sensitivity obeys integral constraints of the form

$$
\int_0^\infty \log |S(j\omega)|\,d\omega
$$

with corrections for unstable poles, nonminimum-phase zeros, and delays. The exact
formula depends on the assumptions, but the design message is persistent:
performance redistribution is unavoidable.

## Intuition

The waterbed effect is the practical picture: pushing sensitivity down at low
frequency often makes it rise near crossover or at higher frequency. Plants with
delay or right-half-plane zeros make the tradeoff harsher.

## How It Is Used

1. Identify delay, unstable poles, and nonminimum-phase zeros before setting
   bandwidth targets.
2. Use Bode plots to inspect gain slope and phase near crossover.
3. Check sensitivity peaks, not only low-frequency error.
4. Treat aggressive specifications as suspect when they violate known loop limits.

## Common Pitfalls

- Asking loop shaping to beat delay or nonminimum-phase behavior by gain alone.
- Ignoring the sensitivity peak created by excellent low-frequency rejection.
- Interpreting Bode relations outside their assumptions without checking a robust
  analysis.
