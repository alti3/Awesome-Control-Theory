## What It Means

The **Nyquist criterion** determines closed-loop stability from the open-loop
frequency response. It is stronger than just reading gain and phase margins because
it accounts for unstable open-loop poles and the full encirclement of the critical
point.

## Mathematical Form

For a negative-feedback loop with loop transfer $L(s)$, closed-loop poles are roots
of

$$
1+L(s)=0.
$$

The Nyquist contour tracks how $1+L(s)$ winds around the origin, equivalently how
$L(s)$ winds around $-1$.

## Intuition

The plot asks whether the loop transfer gets too close to turning negative
feedback into positive feedback at a frequency where the loop still has enough gain
to matter.

## How It Is Used

1. Count unstable open-loop poles.
2. Plot or compute the Nyquist curve for $L(j\omega)$ with the proper contour.
3. Count encirclements of $-1$ using the sign convention of the chosen text or
   software.
4. Infer closed-loop unstable poles and then check margins.

## Common Pitfalls

- Applying the simple margin interpretation to open-loop unstable plants.
- Forgetting delays, which add phase lag and change encirclements.
- Confusing the Nyquist plot with the Nyquist stability criterion.
