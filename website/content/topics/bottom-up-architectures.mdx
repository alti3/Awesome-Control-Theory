## What It Means

**Bottom-up architectures** assemble larger systems from local feedback loops,
filters, selectors, feedforward elements, and coordination logic. This style is
common in process control, industrial automation, and systems where many local
loops were installed before centralized optimization or supervision was added.

## Mathematical Form

A decentralized loop collection has local laws such as

$$
u_i=C_i(r_i,y_i),\qquad i=1,\dots,m,
$$

with a higher layer adjusting setpoints, constraints, or loop pairings.

## Intuition

Local loops make subsystems predictable and maintainable. Their interactions,
however, can create hidden multivariable behavior, so coordination and pairing
choices still matter.

## How It Is Used

1. Stabilize and commission local loops.
2. Analyze interaction through gains, delays, and relative gain array or singular
   values.
3. Add selectors, decouplers, or supervisory optimization when local loops
   compete.
4. Preserve maintainability by documenting ownership and priorities.

## Common Pitfalls

- Assuming stable local loops imply stable system-level behavior.
- Adding decoupling without checking model uncertainty.
- Letting multiple loops manipulate the same actuator without priority logic.
