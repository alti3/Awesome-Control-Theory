## What It Means

**Controller implementation** turns an ideal control law into hardware or software
that runs with finite precision, finite sampling rate, delays, saturating actuators,
noisy sensors, and mode changes. Implementation details matter because many
apparent design failures are caused by realizability details rather than the
nominal controller equation.

## Mathematical Form

Implementation replaces ideal operations with realizable ones, for example

$$
K_d s \quad\rightarrow\quad \frac{K_d s}{1+sT_f},
$$

and continuous integrators with sampled recursions such as

$$
I[k+1]=I[k]+T_s e[k].
$$

## Intuition

The implemented controller is part of the plant from the closed-loop perspective.
Sampling, computation delay, filters, scaling, saturation, and reset logic all
change the loop transfer function.

## How It Is Used

1. Choose sampling time, numerical form, scaling, and filter realizations.
2. Add anti-windup, limit handling, and bumpless transfer.
3. Account for computation and communication delay.
4. Test the exact implementation in simulation, HIL, or staged commissioning.

## Common Pitfalls

- Implementing ideal differentiation or ideal switching.
- Ignoring integer scaling, word length, and overflow.
- Validating a continuous design but deploying a delayed sampled controller.
