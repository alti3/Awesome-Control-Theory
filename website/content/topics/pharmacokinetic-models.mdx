## What It Means

**Pharmacokinetic models** describe how a drug moves through body compartments,
how it is eliminated, and how concentration relates to effect. Drug administration
is a useful example because compartment models, observability, state feedback, and
closed-loop dosing can reduce concentration variability.

## Mathematical Form

A two-compartment model can be written as

$$
\dot x = A x + B u,\qquad y=Cx,
$$

where states represent drug amounts or concentrations, $u$ is infusion or dose
rate, and $y$ is the measured concentration.

## Intuition

The clinically relevant concentration may not be directly measured. Control and
estimation are useful because a dosing input affects hidden compartments through
delayed transport and elimination dynamics.

## How It Is Used

1. Choose compartments and transfer/elimination rates.
2. Decide whether dose input is bolus, infusion, or absorption through another
   compartment.
3. Check observability from available measurements.
4. Design dosing policies while respecting safety limits and patient variability.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing measured blood concentration with active-site concentration.
- Ignoring patient-to-patient parameter variation.
- Designing aggressive feedback without safety constraints.
