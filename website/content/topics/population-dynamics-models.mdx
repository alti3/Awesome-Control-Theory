## What It Means

**Population dynamics models** describe how species, groups, or interacting
populations evolve over time. Logistic growth and predator-prey examples connect
nonlinear modeling, equilibria, phase portraits, and feedback intervention.

## Mathematical Form

A logistic model is

$$
\dot x = r x\left(1-\frac{x}{K}\right),
$$

while predator-prey models introduce coupled states and interaction terms such as
prey consumption and predator growth.

## Intuition

Population feedback is often nonlinear: growth slows near carrying capacity, one
species can regulate another, and parameter changes can shift equilibria or create
oscillations.

## How It Is Used

1. Choose state variables and interaction terms.
2. Find equilibria and local stability.
3. Plot phase portraits to understand cycles and basins.
4. Test interventions, harvesting, or resource changes as control inputs.

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating average deterministic models as reliable for small populations.
- Interpreting negative equilibrium populations as physically meaningful.
- Ignoring parameter uncertainty in ecological interactions.
