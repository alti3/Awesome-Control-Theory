## What It Means

**Fundamental limitations** are performance barriers imposed by the plant and the
implementation environment. These limits keep design work from mistaking tuning
effort for missing authority: unstable poles, right-half-plane zeros, delay, noise,
saturation, and finite actuator bandwidth all restrict what feedback can achieve.

## Mathematical Form

Several limits appear through $S$ and $T$:

$$
S=\frac{1}{1+L},\qquad T=\frac{L}{1+L}.
$$

Reducing $|S|$ in one frequency band can increase it elsewhere, while zeros, poles,
and delays constrain crossover frequency and phase margin.

## Intuition

Feedback redistributes error; it does not remove physics. A controller can make a
plant behave better where the loop has authority, but it must pay with control
effort, noise sensitivity, reduced robustness, or slower response somewhere else.

## How It Is Used

1. Identify nonminimum-phase zeros, unstable poles, delay, saturation, and sensor
   noise early.
2. Estimate feasible bandwidth before selecting a controller family.
3. Check sensitivity peaks and actuator effort against the actual hardware.
4. Relax specifications or redesign hardware when the limits are structural.

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating bandwidth as a software parameter independent of the plant.
- Ignoring actuator rate limits in frequency-domain designs.
- Testing only nominal tracking while missing robustness and noise amplification.
