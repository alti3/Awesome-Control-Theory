## What It Means

**Control-system architectures** describe how controllers, estimators, reference
generators, planners, logic, and optimization layers are connected. Architecture is
a design decision in its own right: a complex system is often made tractable by
nesting loops, separating time scales, and assigning clear authority to each layer.

## Mathematical Form

A common layered pattern is

$$
r_\text{inner}=G_\text{outer}(r,y),\qquad
u=G_\text{inner}(r_\text{inner},y_\text{inner}),
$$

with supervisory logic or optimization modifying references, constraints, and
modes.

## Intuition

Architecture is how a control problem is decomposed. Inner loops make fast
variables predictable; outer loops command slower objectives; estimators provide
state; logic protects the plant; optimization handles constraints.

## How It Is Used

1. Allocate control authority across time scales and physical subsystems.
2. Decide which signals are measured, estimated, commanded, or constrained.
3. Design inner loops before relying on them as simplified blocks in outer loops.
4. Verify interactions between feedback, feedforward, logic, and optimization.

## Common Pitfalls

- Assuming nested loops are independent when their bandwidths overlap.
- Adding supervisory logic without documenting priority and reset behavior.
- Designing a controller before deciding what variables should be controlled.
